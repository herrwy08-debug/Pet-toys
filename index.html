Pet Guides

Quick, practical tips on pet health, food and toys. Tap a headline to read.

Vaccines, parasites and warning signs

Pet Medical

Don’t skip vaccines and parasite prevention

Puppies and kittens need their core vaccines on the schedule your vet recommends, and adult pets need boosters. Flea, tick and worm prevention should be regular too. Ask your vet which products and timing are right.

Signs you should call the vet

Repeated vomiting or diarrhea, not eating or drinking for a day or more, fast or labored breathing, trouble urinating (especially in male cats), seizures, or sudden wobbliness. Don’t wait these out at home.

Never give human medicine

Common human drugs such as ibuprofen and acetaminophen can be toxic to dogs and cats. Always ask your vet before giving any medication, and never guess at a dose.

Checkups, weight and daily care

Pet Health

Yearly checkups

Healthy adult pets should see the vet about once a year. Senior pets may benefit from more frequent visits, so ask your vet what makes sense. Catching problems early makes them easier to treat.

Keep a healthy weight

You should be able to feel your pet’s ribs under a light layer of fat, and see a waistline from above. Extra weight raises the risk of joint problems, diabetes and more.

Don’t forget dental care

Brush your pet’s teeth regularly with pet-safe toothpaste, never human toothpaste. Heavy tartar or bad breath is worth a vet visit.

Choosing, switching and foods to avoid

Pet Food

How to choose a main food

Look for a food labeled “complete and balanced” for your pet’s life stage: puppy or kitten, adult, or senior. A named protein source near the top of the ingredient list is a good sign.

Switch foods slowly

Mix the new food into the old over 7 to 10 days, increasing the new food a little each day. This helps prevent an upset stomach.

Foods to keep away from pets

Chocolate, grapes and raisins, onions, garlic and anything sweetened with xylitol can be dangerous to dogs and cats. If your pet eats any of these, call your vet or an animal poison hotline right away.

Safety, durability and play

Pet Toys

Match toys to size and chew strength

Toys that are too small can be swallowed, and ones that are too hard can damage teeth. Pick toys that fit your pet’s size and chewing style.

Inspect and replace often

Throw away toys that are torn, shedding stuffing or fraying. Loose pieces and strings are easy to swallow.

Playtime beats toy count

Cats love wand toys and small balls that mimic hunting, while dogs enjoy tug and fetch. A little scheduled playtime each day does more than a pile of toys.

This is general information only and not a substitute for veterinary advice. If your pet seems unwell, contact your veterinarian.
